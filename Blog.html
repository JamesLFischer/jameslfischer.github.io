Building a VHS Render Pipeline for Skull World: Renders 01 and 02

November 14, 2026

Skull World is a game I’m building. These two renders are visual direction for it. The pipeline that made them runs locally, on a laptop GPU with 8 GB of VRAM.

The pipeline

Stills come from a custom SDXL LoRA, skullworld_sdxl_v1, trained on about 4,000 captioned frames from my own VHS and art datasets. I generate in ComfyUI. ControlNet locks a 3D skeleton model I built, so the character stays consistent. Photoreal scenes use RealVisXL.

A custom animated VHS filter, written in Python, adds tracking bars, dropouts, head-switch noise, dot crawl, and tape warble. The filter is piped into ffmpeg.

An ownership watermark, the King’s Coin, is baked into the scene before the tape filter. It’s a crowned skull with my name on the rim. Because it goes in first, the tape treatment degrades the mark along with the footage.

Render 01

12 seconds, 768×1024. No AI video model. The motion is a scripted camera move on a still: a push-in, handheld sway, and parallax, rendered frame by frame in Python. The soundtrack, Dead Channel Lullaby, is fully synthesized in code. No samples.

Render 02

12 seconds. The skeleton walks toward the camera in front of a wall of CRTs and says “You shouldn’t have pressed play.”

The skeleton is matted out of the still and animated as a 2.5D cut-out puppet: walk bob, leg stretch, opposite arm swing, a contact shadow, and screen-light flicker on the bones. The background is a RealVisXL-inpainted plate, so the CRTs stay locked. 26 screens are masked to flicker independently, with a rolling scan band and flashes.

The voice is local TTS (Piper), processed in code: a pitch drop, vocal fry, tape wow and flutter, hiss, hum, and footsteps. The jaw is driven by the voice’s loudness envelope, for automatic lip sync.

An earlier attempt used an AI video model (Wan 2.1). The motion wasn’t right, so I moved to the puppet approach.
